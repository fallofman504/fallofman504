How it works

Your ZIP code gives your USDA hardiness zone (2023 map). From the zone, the almanac estimates your last spring frost and first fall frost. Every planting window and date is counted from those two days, the same way extension services publish planting calendars.

Pick a date (today by default) and the almanac lists what can be planted now, what's coming up soon, and everything else. Check the plants you want and press Plant to get a dated schedule: germination, hardening off, transplanting, fertilizing, succession sowings and harvest.

Use it offline / install it

  • iPhone / iPad: open this page in Safari, tap the Share button, then Add to Home Screen.
  • Android: open in Chrome, tap the ⋮ menu, then Install app or Add to Home screen.
  • Computer: use the install icon in the address bar, or download the folder and open index.html.

Good to know

  • Frost dates by zone are averages. Coastal, valley and mountain gardens can differ by 2–3 weeks; set your own dates in the location screen for the best results.
  • Days to harvest vary by variety. Check your seed packet and adjust.
  • Your location and garden are saved only in this browser on this device.

Data sources

USDA Plant Hardiness Zone Map (2023), ZIP code edition, produced by the PRISM Climate Group, Oregon State University. Place names from the open-source zipcodes package. Planting timings compiled from standard university extension planting guides.
